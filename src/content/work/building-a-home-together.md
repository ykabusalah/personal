This was a North Korean refugee that I met through the contacts of one of my friends who worked at a North Korean school. Initially, I treated it as any normal interview, as we relaxed at a local cafe. We spoke of each other’s journeys, our lives up to that point, and slowly I began to see similarities. Similarities in the ways that we thought about life. The way we escaped our homes. Our destiny as providers for our struggling parents. Loss. Fear. The legacy of refugee status. In many ways, I felt like I was looking at a younger version of myself. We both were fond of drawing our dream homes (stability) when we were children.

But as we spoke, I felt like I was rebuilding a home that I hadn’t remembered in a long time; a home of my past dreams. A reminder that I was still who I was back then, and that wasn’t such a bad thing.
